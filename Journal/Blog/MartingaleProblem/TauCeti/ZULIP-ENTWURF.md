Hi all — I am planning a set of four roadmaps and would like to announce them
before opening any pull request, and to ask three things that the README's
"Coordinate first" item tells me to ask here rather than to assume.

**What they are.** Weak convergence of measures, the Kolmogorov extension
theorem, Skorokhod space `D(ι, E)`, and abstract martingale problems. They form
a chain with one fork: `WeakConvergence` and `KolmogorovExtension` sit on Mathlib
alone and are independent of each other; `SkorokhodSpace` rests on
`WeakConvergence`; and `MartingaleProblems` on all three. 33 milestones in total
(6 / 3 / 10 / 14).

The subject is the one Ethier–Kurtz treat: càdlàg paths, tightness and weak
convergence on `D`, the martingale problem as the primitive notion, restart and
uniqueness, duality, and convergence of processes. The design choices that are
genuinely mathematical are written into the roadmaps rather than left to the
implementer — the operator as a *relation* rather than a function, solutions with
respect to an arbitrary filtration as the primitive, a time index that is an
arbitrary preorder carrying a measure (so that discrete and continuous time are
one theory), and `RCLike` test functions.

**Grounding.** Three of the four carry a `Suggested.lean`; together they are
2,927 declarations in about 82,000 lines, and they type check in dependency order
against Mathlib `master` (`94ef6b89544`, Lean `v4.35.0-rc2`) with `autoImplicit`
and `relaxedAutoImplicit` off: 0 errors, 0 `sorry`, 0 deprecated names, and every
declaration reducing to `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`. Every
Mathlib name cited in a README is checked against the source with its file and
line, and the "Mathlib does not have this" claims are re-checked mechanically on
every revision — that check has caught four false negatives of mine so far, all
of them names generated by `@[to_additive]` and therefore absent from every
`theorem` line.

**Plan: four separate pull requests, in dependency order.** I had first intended
one PR for all four, but the sizes talk me out of it. Measured against the 46
roadmaps already here (median 6,172 words, largest 44,371): `KolmogorovExtension`
1,737 words, `WeakConvergence` 25,031, `SkorokhodSpace` 49,260,
`MartingaleProblems` 151,409. Binding the two small ones to the largest would
serve nobody. So I would open `WeakConvergence` and `KolmogorovExtension` first,
then `SkorokhodSpace`, then `MartingaleProblems` — each touching only its own
directory. The last one I am still editing down: it grew alongside the work and
currently reads in places as a research log rather than a specification, and that
is my problem to fix before submitting it, not the reviewers'.

Each roadmap, and all four together, can be seen without a PR:
https://github.com/pfaffelh/TauCetiRoadmap/branches

**Question 1 — coordination.** Two of Rémy Degenne's repositories are cited as
material to *take over* rather than rewrite: `brownian-motion` (the càdlàg basics
and the quasimartingale càdlàg modification) and `kolmogorov_extension4`. Both
are Apache-2.0, so the licence is not the issue; agreement is. @**Rémy Degenne**
— would you be happy for these roadmaps to direct an implementer at that code,
with your copyright headers and attribution kept? I would rather ask than infer
it from the licence.

I should also name prior art I am *not* drawing on: Scott Armstrong's
`MarkovProcess` builds continuous-path Markov processes from a transition
semigroup, with the Dynkin martingale and a Trotter–Kato route to weak
convergence. It contains no càdlàg path and no martingale problem, so there is no
collision, but the overlap in subject is close enough that it should be named
rather than discovered.

**Question 2 — the Mathlib pin.** `SkorokhodSpace/Suggested.lean` imports
`Mathlib.Topology.Order.Cadlag`, which is in Mathlib `master` but not at the
Mathlib revision Tau Ceti currently pins (`dc4b8d6`, 2026-09-04). I built the
branch locally: `WeakConvergence` compiles against the current pin, and the other
two fail on that single missing module and nothing else. How often does the pin
move, and is it worth waiting for, or would you rather I worked around it? I
would rather wait — the alternative is restating Mathlib's `IsCadlag` behind a
stand-in, which is exactly what "Defer to Mathlib" tells me not to do.

**Question 3 — a reviewer.** The README notes that naming a subject-area expert
is often what decides whether a roadmap merges or sits. I am a probabilist and
this is my own area, so I can answer questions on the mathematics at any depth;
what I cannot do is review my own roadmap. If anyone here works in weak
convergence of stochastic processes, or knows someone who does and would not mind
being asked, I would be glad of the introduction.

Thanks — and happy to answer anything.
